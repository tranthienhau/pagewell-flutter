Chapter 2 • Salt and Dust

The Salt Road

The caravan left before dawn, when the salt still held the cold of the night. Nadia walked beside the third camel, counting the bells the way her father had taught her, seven brass, three tin, each ringing a half-beat behind the stride.

Across the vast white basin of the flats, nothing cast a shadow except their own slow march against the fading stars. The crust of mineral crunching beneath her boots felt brittle as glazed sugar, ready to give way to the black brine waiting underneath.

Inside the heavy wool of her traveling coat, the sealed wax of the letter pressed hard against her collarbone. A salt trader's daughter was not supposed to carry correspondence between warring prefectures, yet here she was, her pulse thumping against the parchment.

A dry wind rose off the eastern hills as the horizon turned from plum to pale apricot. It tasted of ancient evaporated seas and bitter rosemary. She pulled her linen veil tighter over her mouth, watching the lead scout raise his arm to signal the morning descent.

Three days remained before they would reach the terraced gates of Oakhaven. Three days to decide whether the parchment stayed hidden, or burned to ash in the dusk campfires.
