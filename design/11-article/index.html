Why we still read by the sea

Clara Bennett 6 min read

On coastal holidays, the clock resets itself against the tide. We pack books we promised ourselves we would finish months ago, carrying them across pebble coves and wooden boardwalks, knowing the rhythm of breaking water makes stillness feel earned rather than idle.

Modern reading is often hurried between notifications and transit stops. But beside open water, attention lengthens. The horizon removes peripheral competition, turning each page into an unhurried, private conversation between the mind and the tide.

“A book by the water asks nothing of you but time.”

Even an hour on a windblown dune leaves salt on the paper and salt in the hair, a quiet reminder that stories, like tides, are best experienced when we surrender to their pace.
